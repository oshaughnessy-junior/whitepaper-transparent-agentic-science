\title{Transparent agentic science: Reproducible evidence and adversarial review}
\author{R.~O'Shaughnessy}
\date{October 23, 2026}
\begin{abstract}
Capable AI agents are effective scientific code developers and operators, particularly at finding bugs and inconsistencies that survived extensive human scrutiny. We describe a living guide built around adversarial review, continuous integration for code changes, and provenance connecting every numerical claim and figure to reproducible computations. Generated result macros prevent transcription drift; independent challenges test scientific and numerical assumptions; executable checks preserve repairs. We specify what each repository should record to obtain inputs, regenerate results, and verify artifacts. Examples come from gravitational-wave population studies and the RIFT development sequence. Human physical intuition, especially inspection of plots, helps identify which discrepancies matter. Scientific writing remains the hardest component: capable models and sustained human direction are needed to establish context, priorities, and a coherent argument. The companion public repository is assembled entirely with AI from prior work with a human; all commits are performed by AI agents.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
AI use and capability in science are expanding rapidly~\cite{Hao2026,Boiko2023}. An agent can inspect code, run a calculation, construct a counterexample, and trace a discrepancy into the figures and claims that depend on it. For scientific developers and operators, a major practical benefit is finding errors in established workflows. Extensive human review should motivate further discriminating tests rather than exempt a result from scrutiny.

The Physical Review, ApJ/AAS, and arXiv policies retain human responsibility for accuracy and attribution while requiring disclosure of substantive AI use~\cite{APS2026,AAS2026,arXivAI}. They address concrete failures: invented results, fictitious references, unchecked figures, and scientific claims copied without verification. Our response is to make the evidence inspectable: execute the calculation, identify its inputs, challenge its assumptions, and preserve the checks that justify the claim.

In this paper, we present a \textbf{living guide to reliable science with agents}. It draws on the main population-inference papers~\cite{SyntheticUniverses,HierarchicalMethods}, the RIFT review sequence and third-generation study~\cite{RIFTThreeG}, and reusable research skills. Unless otherwise noted, we use the latest OpenAI model available at the time at Sol scale, or comparably capable Claude models at Opus scale, and recommend comparable capability for science. Under these conditions, our developer and operator experience is that \textbf{adversarial review already matches or exceeds typical human performance at finding and diagnosing errors in ordinary scientific workflows}. Multiple examples below concern defects that previous scrutiny missed. This is an operational assessment, not a controlled performance measurement.

The working standard has three parts:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Adversarial review:} a fresh reviewer tries to falsify the claim and reproduce a relevant failure.
\item \textbf{Continuous integration (CI):} code checks exercise the actual execution path and prevent diagnosed defects from returning.
\item \textbf{Provenance:} every result, figure, and repository exposes enough information to reproduce the work and verify the artifact.
\end{itemize}
The examples supply inspected development evidence; only the synthetic case is executed here, and the cited scientific campaigns have not been independently rerun for this guide. The scientist supplies the research priorities and physical interpretation; either human or agent can originate an objection. Section~\ref{sec:documentation} describes provenance, Sec.~\ref{sec:reliability} covers review and CI, and Sec.~\ref{sec:literature} addresses citations. Writing, which currently needs the most judgment, is discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:writing}. The appendices provide an executable illustration and supporting skills.

\section{Make every result and figure traceable}\label{sec:documentation}
\textbf{A number enters the paper through a recorded computation.} The analysis writes a result record containing the value, units, uncertainty convention, effective configuration, and input identities. A generator supplies numerical macros, table cells, and figure annotations from that record. Repeated quantities use the same named result in the abstract, body, caption, and conclusion. This adapts provenance practices used in LVK internal review; the public catalog documentation provides a related example of identifiable data products~\cite{GWTCRelease}.

The provenance must describe \emph{what ran}. A comment or directory name can record an intended setting that a launcher never supplied. Outputs therefore retain code identity, local changes, environment, command/configuration, random seeds where relevant, and completion status. Cache keys include calibration and other dependencies; file timestamps do not establish freshness. Optimization records include initialization, restart schedule, checkpoints, and the rule selecting the reported solution.

\textbf{Every figure, including a diagnostic plot, needs a reproducible route.} Its caption explains the scientific content; its linked provenance supplies the reconstruction details:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Inputs:} the exact dataset or immutable release, selection cuts, units, and input hashes.
\item \textbf{Producer:} the plotting script or build target, code revision and local changes, environment, and effective settings.
\item \textbf{Transformations:} filtering, weighting, normalization, coordinates, smoothing, and uncertainty construction.
\item \textbf{Outputs:} the plotted numerical data, generated annotations, and identities of the final figure and its source artifacts.
\end{itemize}
A concise provenance sentence in the caption points to the recipe; source comments and a machine-readable sidecar carry the detailed record. A script name alone is insufficient when its inputs or settings cannot be recovered. Replotting saved data and rerunning the scientific analysis are separate commands, with their scope and cost stated.

\textbf{Every repository must explain how to reproduce and verify its work.} Its entry point links the paper, data, recipes, evidence, and review history. The minimum reproduction record is:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Obtain:} input release identifiers, access instructions, licenses, and checksums; explicitly identify unavailable or restricted inputs.
\item \textbf{Run:} pinned dependencies, effective configuration, representative commands, resource needs, and expected outputs.
\item \textbf{Verify:} test commands, tolerances or invariants, generated-artifact checks, and links to the reviewed revision and CI results.
\end{itemize}
A fresh checkout should be sufficient to follow those instructions when the declared inputs and resources are available. Large outputs can live in an archive, but the repository must identify the archived version. Historical results retain their original sample and a visible explanation of the freeze. Current results must use the inputs declared for the current analysis.

The \texttt{result-provenance} skill supports the result record, macros, caches, and generated artifacts. It complements documentation rather than replacing it: consistent macros can still describe the wrong statistic or run. A clean regeneration check prevents specified forms of drift; scientific review establishes whether the chosen quantity supports the claim.

\section{Challenge results and keep the checks}\label{sec:reliability}
\subsection{Adversarial review}
\textbf{Ask the reviewer to find a failure, not endorse the explanation.} Agents can compare solvers on common inputs, trace the first divergent intermediate quantity, enumerate callers that retain an old default, and identify which run supplied a contradictory figure or statement. Their sustained attention across these connections makes them particularly effective auditors of mature scientific workflows.

A substantive result receives distinct scientific and code/numerics challenges:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Pin the candidate.} Supply the revision, scientific claim, assumptions, units, domain, evidence locations, and acceptance conditions.
\item \textbf{Separate first-pass reviews.} Use fresh contexts; withhold the implementer's advocacy and the other reviewer's conclusions. Record shared inputs, libraries, and assumptions.
\item \textbf{Demand discriminating evidence.} Reproduce a known answer, derive a result by another route, or construct a counterexample. Test boundaries, convergence, and observable consequences relevant to the claim.
\item \textbf{Resolve and recheck.} Preserve findings and their dispositions. After a repair, rerun affected checks and review the changed candidate; a reviewer who implements a repair becomes an author of that change.
\end{itemize}
The \texttt{adversarial-science-review} skill specifies these roles and records. Separate contexts reduce conversational dependence; they do not remove a shared input defect. Self-review is labeled as such. A missing, timed-out, or inconclusive review is not acceptance.

\textbf{Human intuition initiates useful tests.} A plot may reveal an implausible boundary, missing tail, shifted population, or normalization error before anyone can name its cause. The agent can trace that concern through data construction, coordinate transformations, likelihoods, selection, and visualization. The investigator's expectation remains a hypothesis: the agent should also demonstrate when it is wrong. Inspect axes, units, cuts, weights, uncertainty bands, and the distinction between likelihoods and probability densities. An attractive image is not a validation result.

\subsection{Continuous integration for code work}
\textbf{A diagnosed defect should become an executable regression.} CI runs the relevant checks on the candidate change and after integration. Scientific validation must be chosen for the calculation; its recurring mechanical checks belong in CI where feasible:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Exercise the operator's route.} Test parser choices, dispatch, configuration propagation, and the public entry point as well as numerical helpers.
\item \textbf{Check meaningful answers.} Use analytic or planted-truth cases, invariants, boundary conditions, backend comparisons, and appropriate convergence tests.
\item \textbf{Show the test can fail.} Restore the defect in an isolated copy or introduce a relevant mutation. Also retain valid cases so an overaggressive guard cannot masquerade as a repair.
\item \textbf{Inspect what ran.} Report collected, passed, failed, and skipped checks, their environment, and the tested revision. Missing tools or absent required hardware must not produce a reassuring empty result.
\item \textbf{Check the evidence products.} Regenerate macros, tables, and small reference artifacts; detect stale inputs, undefined result keys, and mismatched outputs.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Validate new code against scientific examples and established references.} Unit tests are followed by comparisons that an independent scientist can interpret:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Reproduce the paper examples.} Run the documented demonstrations and representative analysis cases; regenerate their observables, figures, and tables. Check the numerical claims they support, including their uncertainty and stated domain.
\item \textbf{Compare with previous codes.} Run the new and earlier released implementations on matched inputs, physical conventions, settings, and accuracy targets. Explain disagreements; preserve intentional corrections as versioned changes with regression evidence.
\item \textbf{Use external benchmarks.} Reproduce analytic results and benchmark calculations from the literature with their published assumptions and error budgets. Identify the reference, implementation, and any shared dependencies so agreement is meaningful.
\item \textbf{Exercise production environments.} Validate the installed executable and normal launch/configuration route on the intended CPU/GPU backend, dependency stack, scheduler, and representative data. Include a bounded end-to-end case and comparison with a baseline; record missing coverage and obtain the full production rerun when needed.
\end{itemize}
A fast CI fixture should preserve a small version of each practical regression where possible. Expensive paper reproductions and production campaigns retain archived commands, environment identities, raw outputs, tolerances, and a receipt linked from the change. Passing a helper test on a laptop does not establish that the deployed workflow reproduces the paper or the benchmark. An older code is a comparator, not an infallible oracle: resolve a disagreement through independent evidence rather than forcing agreement with a known defect.

The \texttt{simulation-validation} skill supports known-answer tests, convergence studies, negative controls, and test-count checks. CI supplies repeatable evidence for its tested domain. Expensive production reruns, inaccessible data, and hardware-specific checks may require separately archived runs; their status remains explicit. A green CI badge does not certify an untested scientific claim.

For stochastic calculations, vary both seeds and sample size. A well-mixed sampler can still explore an unidentified parameter or a prior boundary. A global fit improvement can conceal failure in the population motivating the claim. The hierarchical-modeling paper illustrates boundary limitations and detected-population tests~\cite{HierarchicalMethods}; the synthetic-universe paper treats an additional component as a hypothesis where numerical support cannot sustain an evidence comparison~\cite{SyntheticUniverses}. These questions belong in the validation plan before a striking number reaches the abstract.

\subsection{Code examples: RIFT and the matter backend}
The RIFT PR sequence records diagnosis, repair, review, and regression evidence in established scientific software:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{An algebraically harmless offset caused underflow.} A batch-wide likelihood maximum suppressed valid lower-likelihood rows in floating-point arithmetic. The repair used per-sample offsets and tested a square-array case that could conceal incorrect broadcasting~\cite{RIFTBatch}.
\item \textbf{Passing tests missed numerical and wiring defects.} Hermite quadrature review found invalid handling of legitimate non-finite rows, an incorrect endpoint claim, a vacuous fixture, and a method unreachable through its parser or dispatch. Reintroducing the defects made the repaired checks fail~\cite{RIFTHermite}.
\item \textbf{A shared rule name concealed different backends.} CPU and GPU selected different quadratures for the same sampled likelihood. The repair unified selection and tested the old disagreement by mutation~\cite{RIFTBackend}.
\end{itemize}
The third-generation RIFT paper gives the scientific context: loud sources, narrow likelihood peaks, and detector-response effects make these numerical distinctions consequential~\cite{RIFTThreeG}. Later PRs must retain their own version identity rather than be attributed automatically to the posted paper. The linked records document the original checks; this guide does not claim new reruns of those campaigns.

\textbf{The nuclear-matter backend shows why independent solvers and production tests matter.} In \href{https://github.com/oshaughnessy-junior/NuclearMatter-Backend}{NuclearMatter-Backend} (\texttt{nmbackend}), an independent radius-domain stellar-structure solver exposed under-resolution in an enthalpy-domain default and motivated an adaptive implementation. Subsequent comparisons with RePrimAnd and other reference routes distinguish solver error from interpolation and surface conventions~\cite{MatterBackend}. Value agreement alone does not validate derivatives: the development record calls for separate automatic-differentiation and finite-difference checks at the derivative order used by the analysis.

Read-only project lessons from CIT, cardassia, and sporc add an operational test: \textbf{verify the code actually imported and the workflow actually completed}. Relevant failures include repeated JAX compilation under a production resource limit, an environment importing a different checkout, a shared branch moving between validation and delivery, and a completed long job whose follow-on step never ran. These records motivate import-path and revision checks, repeated-call tests, scheduler-aware execution receipts, and revalidation on the delivered revision. They are development lessons from restricted sources, not newly reproduced campaigns or claims that all remaining validation gaps are closed.

The population-study development records similarly exposed mis-keyed sample caches and launcher defaults that disagreed with the claimed physical configuration. Independent software evidence includes AI-generated fuzz targets finding defects in OpenSSL and in a cJSON function that already had a human-written harness~\cite{AIFuzzing}. These examples motivate using agents to expand discriminating coverage even after extensive previous scrutiny.

\textbf{A retraction must propagate as thoroughly as a repair.} Preserve the failed claim and its defeating test in the research record, identify what remains valid, and sweep dependent figures, tables, captions, conclusions, and related papers. Repeat the checks after the final human or collaborator edit. Notes retain the investigation; the manuscript presents the supported argument.

\section{Cite real and relevant previous work}\label{sec:literature}
\textbf{A reference must exist and support its attached claim.} Agents should use the same discovery and citation tools as careful human researchers: ADS, arXiv, DOI resolution, publisher records, and reference managers. Import or verify the bibliographic metadata, then read the relevant equation, result, or discussion in context. A genuine paper can still be cited for a finding it never established.

Simple checks catch duplicate or undefined citation keys and metadata mismatches. Coverage needs scientific judgment: follow competing methods, terminology, and citation chains, including relevant work that a generated list omits. The AAS guidance explicitly recommends conventional literature tools and direct checks of suggested references~\cite{AAS2026}. The canonical \texttt{bibtex-manager} and \texttt{paper-writing-process} skills support bibliography verification and claim promotion; the local \texttt{human-directed-science-writing} skill requires source support for attributions.

Repository links identify supporting evidence even while access is restricted. They do not make that evidence publicly reproducible. Public preprints, released inputs, executable recipes, and archived outputs each establish a different part of the chain; state which are available.

\section{Writing still requires the most judgment}\label{sec:writing}
\textbf{An excellent debugger can still write a poor paper.} Agents generate fluent text rapidly, but often dwell on minutiae, miss the context and main contribution, repeat caveats, or narrate runs instead of developing an argument. These are failures of priorities and understanding. More prose and sentence polishing do not resolve them.

Use the model capability stated in the Introduction and reserve substantial revision time. The human supplies the question, audience, contribution, comparisons, claim strength, and exclusions. Revision proceeds in this order:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Set the thrust.} Explain why the question matters and what the evidence establishes. Decide what belongs in the paper, appendix, or research notes.
\item \textbf{Repair the argument.} Connect the introduction, formalism, comparisons, limitations, and conclusion. Remove material that does not help the reader assess the contribution.
\item \textbf{Edit the prose.} Shorten and clarify while preserving numbers, units, uncertainty, notation, citation scope, and claim strength. Treat a stronger conclusion as a scientific revision.
\end{itemize}
The \texttt{human-directed-science-writing} skill supplies the brief and semantic audit. The canonical \texttt{paper-style} and \texttt{paper-writing-process} packages support manuscript structure and distillation from notes. In this guide, selective boldface and lists make the decisions easy to locate; connected prose supplies their reasoning. The author remains responsible for an argument they can defend under questioning.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}
The practical standard is \textbf{challenge, test, and trace}. At the model capability assumed here, our experience is that adversarial agent review matches or exceeds typical human performance at finding and diagnosing errors, including errors that survived extensive human vetting. Fresh review finds failures; CI preserves executable checks; provenance connects every reported artifact to its producing work. Together these make claims inspectable and reproduction possible when the declared inputs and resources are available.

Human oversight establishes priorities and physical interpretation, while agents provide persistent scrutiny and reproducible diagnoses. Writing remains the largest continuing demand on judgment. This living guide and its skills retain the evidence and improve the procedure as new lessons emerge.
