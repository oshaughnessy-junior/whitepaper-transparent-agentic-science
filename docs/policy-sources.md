# Official policy sources for the scientific introduction

Verified the APS and AAS guidance on November 19, 2026.

- APS journals require authors to keep a record of AI use and disclose substantive uses in the paper, including material code generation or debugging, derivations, numerical work, and scientific interpretation. Describe the tool and version, its role, and how the authors directed and verified the work. Describe research use in the methods and AI figure generation in the caption as well. Light text editing is exempt from publication disclosure. Editors may ask for additional documentation. The reviewer policy requires disclosure to the editor beyond light editing and restricts use of confidential review material. [APS policy and FAQ](https://journals.aps.org/authors/appropriate-use-ai-tools) · [PRD author guidance](https://journals.aps.org/prd/authors)
- AAS journals ask authors to declare whether AI or LLMs were used in manuscript preparation, including supervised editing and accessibility assistance, and to identify the tool and purpose. Its author guidance covers manuscript preparation and gives figure-specific advice about including prompts when available. Its referee guidance requires a full reading, confidentiality, and disclosure of AI assistance. [AAS author guidelines](https://journals.aas.org/author-llm-guidelines) · [AAS referee guidelines](https://journals.aas.org/referee-llm-guidelines) · [AAS announcement](https://journals.aas.org/news/new-guidelines-on-ai-use-in-aas-journals/)
- arXiv guidance addresses author responsibility, reporting significant tool use, and authorship. Last checked for this repository on October 23, 2026. [arXiv generative-AI guidance](https://info.arxiv.org/help/moderation/index.html#policy-for-authors-use-of-generative-ai-language-tools)

The [policy statements](suggestions-for-policy-statements.md) propose how to implement workflow-level disclosure and preserve evidence in a research group. Check current venue requirements before submitting.
