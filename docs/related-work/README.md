# Related work: evidence and positioning

Research cutoff: **2026-10-25**. This is a selective, source-checked survey, not a systematic review or an independent replication of the cited systems. Three delegated research lanes and root astronomy research used public primary sources. The accepted manuscript baseline is `27968a8`; title, abstract and every original line are preserved.

The useful comparison is among **tasks and evidence**, rather than a single ranking of autonomy. This guide concerns verifiable human–agent physical science. Automated ML experimentation, algorithm search, biological hypothesis generation, document assessment and paper replication provide different evidence of capability. Historical benchmarks cannot calibrate the guide’s current-model bug-finding assessment; that assessment remains explicitly grounded in developer/operator experience.

## Evidence that qualifies a broad success claim

- The systems packet records AI Scientist workshop-review wording: favorable first-round review is narrower than completed publication; this paper makes no workshop-acceptance claim.
- AgentRxiv’s original team reports code/output and report-consistency failures alongside improvement. The paper does not transfer those historical failure rates to current models.
- METR’s early-2025 productivity experiment and its 2026 follow-up are retained together in the verification packet. They measure a different task; the follow-up identifies selection/accounting problems and does not establish a clean causal reversal. Neither is used to infer scientific-review performance.
- ReplicationBench’s abstract and body disagree on the headline percentage. The qualitative design is relevant; the inconsistent number is excluded.
- Co-Scientist’s public metadata contains different online/version dates. The manuscript cites the versioned arXiv record, avoiding an unresolved publication-date claim.
- Prism’s live introduction page includes a later-dated banner. The banner is not evidence about the research cutoff, and Prism is not cited in the manuscript.
- Kosmos engineering and Anthropic evaluation-leakage reports are useful operational context, retained in the practice packet without inflating their status to independent scientific-validation studies.

## Limits and reusable-method radar

No systems were rerun for this survey, and no private run record is released. Primary author/lab reports are attributed as such. Some full supplements and the recent biological preprint were inaccessible; the relevant boundaries are recorded rather than filled by inference. Robin, Virtual Lab, Paper2Agent and other systems are outside this bounded pass; omission is not evidence against them.

Thirteen candidate methods are recorded under `methods/`: claim-class audits, code/report consistency, staged executable checks, fresh reproduction, result provenance, two citation checks, scoped certificates, bounded handoffs, leakage audits, writing ownership, masked replication and target/evidence matrices. Overlapping entries preserve their source-specific assumptions; they are not thirteen independent inventions. All are **test-proposed**, none adopted or asserted field-novel. Each states a cheap future test and constraints.

The public-identifier tool verified **17 of 17** DOI/arXiv entries, with no mismatch or unresolved identifier. The course and two first-party website reports were checked directly. The claim ledger separately records semantic support; passing a metadata check does not certify a scientific statement. The Nature AI-research article has March 25 online metadata and March 26 Crossref issue-date metadata; the manuscript uses only the unambiguous year and volume/pages.
