# Suggestions for policy statements on AI use in physics and astronomy

Draft language for a physics and astronomy program, research group, or journal. The program statements can be used on their own. The research language describes what to preserve as the work proceeds. The journal proposal calls for a change in policy.

## Undergraduate program

> Students will learn where AI can help them develop understanding and verify work, including debugging analysis code, checking units and limiting cases, and testing against lab data. Follow the course or assessment rules and do assigned independent work yourself. When AI assistance is permitted, be able to explain your reasoning, document the checks relevant to your result, follow course rules on disclosure, and handle unpublished or confidential material only in authorized tools.

## Graduate program and capstone research

> Students will learn to use AI tools under human direction, including to build research software, design independent checks, and automate validation and verification. Researchers are responsible for their methods, results, references, and interpretation. Keep a lab notebook that links material decisions and checks to versioned code, inputs, configurations, and outputs so others with appropriate access can assess or reproduce the work. Discuss how you work and handle data with your advisor or research group. Follow the applicable rules for theses, dissertations, and manuscripts.

## Research record and retention

> Keep a lab notebook as the work proceeds. Record decisions when they change an analysis or its interpretation, and preserve the code, inputs, effective settings, outputs, and checks that support the result. Keep failed tests and consequential revisions with the affected calculation. Routine conversations, emails, agent sessions, and histories of which assistant or model version took part are working material. Preserve an instruction or a model setting when it is itself an input to the reported science.

A benchmark is part of the run or code review that used it. A failed convergence check belongs with the calculation it changed. When a comparison changes the interpretation, record the decision and its rationale while they are fresh. Commercial service terms and institutional records rules still apply.

## Commercial tools and confidential material

> Commercial tools may be used when their terms, configuration, and the data involved meet applicable institutional, funder, collaboration, and publication requirements. Before sending unpublished or confidential material, verify access, training-use, and retention terms, including any applicable zero-data-retention arrangement. If the service lacks an explicit zero-data-retention commitment, submit such material only when the responsible data or manuscript stewards approve and governing rules permit it. Peer review remains subject to the journal’s confidentiality and tool rules.

## What journals should change

> Replace standalone AI-use disclosure requirements with ordinary scientific reporting. Report a model and its relevant settings as part of the methods when its output enters the science, for example when it classifies observations or selects the sample. Routine coding assistance requires no separate declaration under this proposal. Evaluate the claims, calculations, figures, and writing on their scientific merits. Authors remain responsible for the paper.

Even an accurate disclosure can only describe assistance and summarize checks. Researchers test code while writing it, compare outputs while choosing settings, and revisit figures while deciding what the result supports. For a reader to assess whether a check was adequate, the calculation, the comparison, and its outcome need to be recorded together as part of that work.

An editor who sees an unsupported conclusion should ask which analysis supports it. A referee who finds the argument hard to follow should identify the passage. Both questions apply to every paper. An estimated fraction of AI-written prose answers neither.

## Current journal rules under critique

APS asks authors to keep a record of AI use and disclose substantive use in the paper, including code generation or debugging that materially affects results. APS asks authors to describe the tool and version, its role, and author direction and verification. Research use belongs in the methods as well, and AI-generated figures should be addressed in their captions. Light text editing is exempt from disclosure. Editors may request further documentation. APS also gives reviewers separate disclosure and confidentiality rules. [APS Journals policy and FAQ](https://journals.aps.org/authors/appropriate-use-ai-tools)

AAS asks authors to declare at submission whether they used AI or LLMs in preparing the manuscript and to identify the tool and purpose, including supervised editing or accessibility assistance. The reason for using an accessibility aid is private. Its author guidelines cover manuscript preparation. For illustrative images and data visualizations, they give caption guidance that can include the prompt when available. Its referee guidelines require reading the submission and protecting confidentiality. They also ask referees to declare LLM use.
